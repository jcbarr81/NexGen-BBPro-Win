- **Deadline-aware CPU trading:** contenders buy (chase veterans), rebuilders
- **CPU-to-CPU trades:** CPU teams now trade with each other, not just with you
- **In-season callups + September expansion:** CPU teams promote AAA prospects

- Player profiles (7.44.6): the strikeout column (K) now shows for every hitter and pitcher; it read a field the simulation never writes, so it was blank for everyone. - Simulation (7.44.6): pinch runners are now used only for the tying or go-ahead run. Bench speedsters had been sent in for almost any slow runner from the 7th inning on, even with the team trailing by eight; over 20 simulated days bench appearances fell from 2.16 to 0.94 per team-game, and specialists who were used in about 85% of games now appear in 15-45% of them. Those extra pinch runners had been worth about 0.08 runs a game, so the overall run environment is nudged back up to compensate (offense_scale 1.015 to 1.0175); league scoring stays where it was and the calibration gates pass on both reference seeds.
- Simulation (7.45.0): **power now hits for power.** The Power rating had almost no effect on home runs while Contact drove them (HR rate vs Power r = -0.06, vs Contact +0.77 on alpha-test), so high-average, ordinary-power hitters posted sluggers' numbers. Contact now decides whether and how squarely the ball is met; Power decides how hard it is hit, with a curve that leaves average hitters average and lets genuine sluggers separate. League scoring and home-run totals are unchanged; who hits the home runs is not. On a 30-day sim of alpha-test, HR vs Power went from +0.01 to +0.45 and the home-run leaders went from contact hitters with power ratings of 48-52 to sluggers rated 60-63. The calibration harness now gates that ratings drive the outcomes they name.
- Fix (7.45.1): a file-permission helper written for Windows (`os.chmod(path, stat.S_IWRITE)`) stripped read and execute access from every league data directory on Linux, so nothing inside could be opened. Production was unaffected only because Cloud Run runs as root; it is what crashed the calibration check on GitHub. It now adds owner-write and leaves the rest of the mode alone. The calibration CI check also now runs at all: it had failed every one of its 64 runs since January, first on committed scratch symlinks under tmp/, then on a stale requirements.txt name, then on this.
- Fix (7.45.2): drafted players no longer disappear from rosters. A shared ownership registry for drafted players was kept once per server process instead of once per league, and draft ids repeat across leagues (D20260013 exists in every 2026 draft), so another league's draft decided who owned alpha-test's picks and they were silently dropped on roster load. Each league now has its own registry, the roster files are the authority on who a player plays for, and entries naming teams from other leagues are purged.
- Rosters (7.45.3): the CPU never trims an owner's team. When a deadline passes, an owner's roster is only fixed where it is illegal ("fill gaps"); nobody is released unless the owner runs Auto-reassign themselves. And no player from this year's draft class is released just for room on any team: auto-assign releases the weakest non-draftee instead, or keeps the pick and leaves the team over the limit.
- Trades (7.45.4): trading a minor leaguer now takes him off his old team. The trade screen offers AAA, Low-A and injured players, but the trade only removed the player from the old team's active roster, so he ended up listed by both clubs (HOU's Erich Zenk was on HOU's and BAL's AAA rosters for a month after HOU traded him). For a traded draft pick it was worse: the next roster reload dropped him from his new team and the trade was silently undone. Draft picks in a trade are also now transferred before any roster changes, so a rejected pick cannot leave a half-applied trade behind, and the transaction log records the level the player actually left.
- Reliability (7.45.5): copied files are now saved to cloud storage. The cloud server decided what to save by comparing each file's modified time with the time of the last save, and a copied file keeps its source's time, so a copy was invisible to it: the season-end archive (last season's players, stats, playoffs and draft files), a cloned league, and the start-of-season roster snapshot would have existed only on the running server and vanished at the next restart. It now tracks every file's modified time and size since its last save and saves any file that is new or different. The server also saves anything still unsaved when it shuts down for a deploy or restart, unless a sim or other background job is mid-write, in which case it leaves the stored league as it was rather than save it half-updated.
- Simulation (7.45.6): **big-league games are played by the active roster.** Since 3.2.12 a game's player pool was the whole organisation -- AAA, Low-A and injured players included -- so the default lineup was the best nine hitters at any level and benches and bullpens ran 20+ deep. League creation wrote those lineups to disk, so on alpha-test ten CPU clubs started three to five minor leaguers in every game of the season, and a player on the 15-day DL played all 71. Games now use the 25-man active roster only; injured players are never eligible, and healthy minor leaguers fill in only if the active roster cannot field nine hitters or a pitcher. A saved lineup that names someone not on the active roster is rebuilt from the active roster before the game, as before.
- Pitching staff (7.45.6): a role held by a pitcher who can't pitch -- traded, released, sent down or hurt -- now shows as vacant on the Lineups page, with who held it and why, instead of keeping him in the slot. Trades never updated the old team's staff, so seven alpha-test clubs still listed a pitcher they no longer had, and saving the staff failed until the owner found and removed him.
- Pitchers (7.45.7): a pitcher's SP/RP now reads the same on every page. players.csv's stored role column is left over from an old endurance-only rule and says "RP" for almost every pitcher in existing leagues (492 of 494 on alpha-test, starters included). The roster page already ignored it, but the player profile, Free Agency and the players list showed it as-is, so a starter could be SP on one page and RP on the next. They now derive it like the roster page: the pitcher's declared role first, then endurance. The dashboard's bullpen card also counted every rotation starter as a reliever for the same reason; it now follows your pitching staff.
- Box scores (7.45.8): box score links are now stored relative to the league (`boxscores/season/<game>.html`) instead of as the server's own file path (`/work/data/leagues/<league>/data/...`). A league that is cloned, restored on another machine or moved to a new storage mount keeps working box score links. Links already stored in the old form keep working too: the box score viewer finds the file by its place in the league's box score folder, wherever the path came from.
- Sims (7.45.9): **a sim day can no longer stall on "Player X is not on the active roster."** When a player got hurt, the game promoted whoever stood first in AAA -- a pitcher on most CPU clubs -- so active rosters slowly turned into 8 hitters and 17 pitchers. Below nine hitters the lineup fill reached across the league for other teams' minor leaguers, the game rejected them, and the day stopped on every retry, losing the rest of that day's games. Fixed at every step:
  - **Injuries use the depth chart first** (commissioner's rule). The next healthy player behind the injured one who is already active takes his lineup spot; only that spot changes. An owner's open roster spot stays open for the owner. A call-up happens for an owner's club only if nobody active can play the position, and then only someone who can. CPU clubs call up like for like (a hitter for a hitter) from their own organisation, and send that same player back down when the regular returns.
  - **Every club gets a depth chart.** Clubs that never saved one get one generated at the next sim (17 of alpha-test's 20). A chart a person saved is never changed; a generated one guides injury coverage but does not pick starters.
  - **Emergency call-ups only from your own organisation**, recorded, when a club can't field nine position players -- before the day's games, owner clubs included, so a game is never unplayable.
  - **CPU clubs keep sound active rosters**: at least 12 position players, a healthy catcher, full and under the cap, every move recorded. CPU clubs also bring players back from the 60-day list (nobody ever did).
  - **A day that fails partway** keeps the games it finished, and the retry plays only the missing ones (it used to drop them and could double-count stats). A retried day no longer skips a starter's turn.
  - Automatic call-ups and send-downs now appear in the Transactions log; loading a roster no longer silently refills it.
  - Fixed: pitcher rest and rotation state was shared by every league on the same server; secondary positions stored as "SS|3B" or "['RF']" weren't recognised.
- Stats (7.45.10): caught stealing, pickoffs, putouts and catcher's interference are no longer double-counted. Batting and fielding stats shared four names in season stats, so every caught stealing was stored twice (league SB% read .718; the real figure is .836), catchers showed 40-plus "caught stealing" as runners, and a first baseman's putouts landed in "picked off". Fixed for games from this release on; stats already recorded this season stand (commissioner decision).
- Box scores (7.45.10): line scores show every inning -- extra-inning games used to stop at the 9th, so the inning runs didn't add up to the final score. Save opportunities are now saves plus blown saves (they were counted at every save-situation entry).
- Fielding credit (7.45.10): pitchers no longer get an assist on every strikeout (a strikeout is the catcher's putout), bunt outs are credited to whoever fielded the bunt instead of always the shortstop, and the double-play pivot man gets his assist. Fielding totals only -- no game results change. Season fielding totals mix the old and new crediting for the rest of 2026.
- OVR (7.45.10): the overall rating now reflects how players actually perform. Hitters are rated mainly on contact and power, plus a little eye, speed and a position-weighted defense value (shortstops and centre fielders count most); pitchers on control, movement and the pitches they actually throw -- a pitcher no longer gains rating just for having more pitches. CPU roster moves and trades use the same rating, so CPU teams now value pitchers on the same scale as hitters. Expect OVR and stars to change; in leagues with tightly bunched ratings the raw score is shown rather than a stretched one. Prospect promotions are unchanged.
- Ballparks (7.45.10): a real ballpark's dimensions apply only when that park was chosen for the team, not because a generated stadium name happens to match a real park ("Royals Stadium" was getting Kauffman Stadium's 1973-93 fences). Applies to new leagues; existing teams keep their current parks.

- Tooling (7.45.11, audit Release 2; no change to game results): the calibration check now measures far more of what the engine audit found. Steal benchmarks are corrected to MLB's real rate (they were about 2x), the 30- and 40-homer counts are realistic and can fail on the low side, and new metrics cover hard-hit, barrel and sweet-spot rates and extra bases taken. About 100 report-only KPIs (bullpen usage, swing rates by count, walk/strikeout spread, velocity fade, run expectancy by base-out state, runs per inning, late & close, platoon by hand, home-field edge, team talent spread) each come with an MLB reference. Known misses are reported on every run without failing it, so each fix release can be measured. A second test league, `data/calibration_league`, is built the way a real league is (league creation, auto-assign, pitching and lineup auto-fill) and runs in CI as report-only. Each plate appearance's first pitch-log entry now records the inning, outs, runners and score; a new test shows simming one day at a time loses bullpen rest (fixed in Release 3); and the injury check now counts injured-list stints only, which shows pitchers land on the IL about 9x less often than in MLB.
- **Rosters (7.46.0): MLB's 26-man active roster.** Teams now carry up to 26 active players, with at most 13 pitchers (28 and 14 from September 1). The organisation limit is 51 (26 active + 15 AAA + 10 Low-A; injured lists don't count), so nobody has to release anyone. Every league switches over automatically; a 25-man roster is still legal.
  - **Your team:** nothing is moved for you. The Roster page shows the limits in force today, your pitcher and position-player counts, and a "spot is open" badge (for example ACT 25/26); the Season page lists the open spot under "Needs your attention", and you get a notify-only "Active roster spot open" notification when you sim. Fill it by promoting from AAA or signing a free agent.
  - **13-pitcher limit:** calling up, signing or trading for a 14th active pitcher goes through with a warning, so you can promote first and option someone next. A roster swap that leaves 14 is refused, and the next game won't start until you're back to 13. "Fill gaps only" fixes it by optioning a reliever (never a rotation starter, never a release). A pitcher coming off the injured list onto a full staff goes to AAA.
  - **CPU clubs** build 13 pitchers / 13 position players from their own AAA and Low-A before each game day and once at Opening Day, never touch an owner's roster, and stop entirely if team ownership can't be read. Every move is in the Transactions log.
  - **September:** rosters may expand to 28 with up to 14 pitchers. When the playoffs begin, every team (owners included) is optioned back to 26 and 13 pitchers, never released; if AAA is full, a young player goes to Low-A instead.
  - **Pitching staff:** still 11 slots. The 12th and 13th pitchers are extra relievers (the Lineups page lists them); more relief slots come with the bullpen update (Release 3).
  - **Offseason:** only CPU clubs promote prospects automatically; yours get a news suggestion and the decision is yours.
  - **New leagues** start with 26-man rosters (13/13), 51-player organisations and editor-valid pitching staffs.
  - **Auto-assign:** a full reassign of an organisation over 51 releases only the players over the limit; anyone else without a slot is parked in AAA and shown as overflow for you to trim. "Fill gaps only" may promote your best AAA bat into an open active spot when AAA is over its cap.
  - **New tutorial:** Help → Tutorials → "26-Man Active Roster". The Roster tutorial, manual and owner guide are updated.
  - Fixed along the way: September swaps on a 27-28-man roster were rejected as over a 25-man cap; the commissioner's "CPU-fill this team" could release an owner's players (it now only fixes what is illegal); "auto-assign all teams" now reassigns CPU teams only and reports the owner teams it skipped; the September revert could option an owner's only healthy catcher; a CPU club with a full organisation could keep outbidding an owner for a free agent it could not sign, so the negotiation never closed; the CPU free-agent market and yearly promotions could act on owners' teams if ownership couldn't be read.
  - **Owner safety (all CPU automation):** if the league's list of owners can't be read for a moment, the CPU now does nothing that day instead of treating every club as CPU-run. That covers roster moves, free-agent bids and negotiations, injured-list activations, the draft pick clock, trade replies and offseason arbitration. The owners list is also saved in one step, so it can never be read half-written.
- **Release 3 (7.47.0): bullpens, rest, injuries and game rules.**
  - **Bullpens work like MLB's.** Relievers pitch by their staff role (any MR slot is a middle reliever; before, many were treated as starters and threw 60-90 pitches in relief). Tired pitchers come out at the start of an inning; a closer who is resting or has pitched two days running is not used, and closers are no longer burned in the middle innings. Relievers other than the closer appear in at most about half the team's games. When the whole bullpen has pitched, a starter rested 2+ days can pitch a short emergency outing (capped around 45 pitches) without losing his next start. Measured: starts of 120+ pitches and relief outings of 60+ pitches are now almost nonexistent, and no closer pitches a third straight day.
  - **Pitching staff:** two new optional slots, MR4 and MR5, for the 12th and 13th pitchers (11 slots still required; your saved staff is unchanged; Auto-fill uses them).
  - **Rest is saved and counted in calendar days.** Bullpen and hitter rest used to live only in the server's memory, so simming one day at a time let relievers pitch every day. It is now saved with each league, so one-day, weekly and multi-day sims give the same results, and an off day counts as rest.
  - **MLB-style schedules:** new leagues and new seasons play series back to back with a weekly off day and the All-Star break (about 190 days instead of 230-275). Current seasons keep their schedules.
  - **Playoffs have dates:** an off day between rounds and travel days inside 5- and 7-game series, so rotations turn over and relievers rest in October.
  - **Extra innings, modern rules:** from the 10th inning each regular-season half-inning starts with a runner on second (his run is unearned). On for every league; commissioners can turn it off in Admin -> Commissioner -> Game rules (from the next game). No more tied games: the old 18-inning tie is gone, and a playoff game is never recorded as a tie.
  - **Rules fixes:** a run no longer scores on an inning-ending double play, and a runner on second is no longer erased on a ground out.
  - **Pitcher injuries look like real baseball, at about 3/4 of MLB's rate.** Each outing carries a small arm-injury chance that rises with pitch count and short rest and falls with durability. Expect about 9-10 pitcher IL stints per team per season (it was about 1). Injuries are decided after the final out, so they never change a game's result. The "Low" injury setting halves the rate. The old cap that quietly turned a 6th injured pitcher into a day-to-day injury is gone.
  - **Tired regulars:** players wear down over a long run of games (catchers fastest) and a day off clears it. Catchers sit on their 4th straight game. A regular who can't be rested because nobody can play his position plays only slightly worse; the full penalty and a small extra injury risk apply when an owner switches Auto rest days off.
  - **New Game Day settings for owners** (My Team -> Settings): Auto rest days (default on), Rest substitutes at similar positions (default on), and automatic activation from the 10/15-day IL (default: the league setting) and from the 60-day IL (default off). If a returning player has no room he waits in AAA and the Season page shows a "make room" item; nothing else on your roster is moved for you. CPU clubs always rest, substitute and activate automatically.
  - **Fewer sim pauses, and roster alerts that work.** The lineup, pitching-staff and roster-limit alerts never actually fired before; they now do, once per problem. By default only serious events pause a sim: a 60-day IL placement, a season-ending injury, an invalid lineup, or an active roster over its limit (minor-league overflow and 10/15-day IL placements notify only). Settings you changed are kept.
  - **CPU rosters:** CPU clubs carry two catchers plus a spare shortstop and centre fielder from their own minors; new leagues give every club a second catcher.
  - **Lineups:** auto-fill picks the best fit for an open position (a 2B or 3B at short, never a non-catcher behind the plate while a catcher is active). If one of your saved lineups lists a player no longer on your active roster, only that lineup (vs LHP or vs RHP) is rebuilt and you get a news item.
  - **Injured-list returns happen on the right day.** IL activations, monthly CPU call-ups and free-agent negotiation days now run after every simulated day, not once per sim call, so a healed player no longer waits until a month-long sim (or the whole "To Playoffs" run) ends, and one-day and month-long sims give the same results. Healed players also come off the IL during the postseason, honouring your settings.
  - **Fixes:** the scheduled deadline honours each owner's IL choices; exhibition games no longer injure players; entering the playoffs always saves the bracket (leagues with a custom playoff format could start the postseason without one); Reset to Opening Day keeps the league's playoff format; a CPU-to-CPU trade can no longer leave a club with 14 active pitchers; player and roster files are written atomically; parallel day simulation matches a serial day exactly; bullpen and hitter rest no longer reset when a season runs past New Year.
- **Release 4 (7.48.0): the running game.**
  - **Steals at MLB's level.** Teams stole about twice as often as MLB clubs (1.4-1.75 steals per team per game); now about 0.7, at a .77-.80 success rate. Speed matters a lot more, and it is read against your league's average runner: a typical runner steals a handful of bases a season, a fast (70) runner about two dozen and a burner (85) about 45, with the best seasons in the 60s. Pitchers who hold runners and strong-armed catchers cut both attempts and success. Runner speed and the battery's ratings (pitcher hold, arm and control; catcher arm and fielding) are read against your league's own averages, computed once a season, so every league steals, and throws wild pitches, at MLB's rate however its ratings happen to be spread.
  - **Steal Frequency slider rebased:** 1.0 now means MLB volume (it ran 1-5 with 3.0 as normal). A saved custom value is converted once.
  - **Rules fixes on the bases:** no more steals on foul balls (about a quarter of all steals happened on fouls); a double steal can no longer produce two outs (the trailing runner takes 2nd without a stolen base when the lead runner is thrown out at 3rd); wild pitches and passed balls only happen with runners on and only count when a runner actually moves.
  - **Wild pitches and passed balls at MLB rates** (about 0.33 and 0.05 per team per game; they were 0.5 and 0.33). Wild control means more wild pitches; a poor-fielding catcher allows more passed balls.
  - **Dropped third strikes follow the rules:** the batter can run only with first base open or two outs (he could reach even with first occupied), he is not always safe, reaches are about a third as common, a batter thrown out is the first baseman's putout and the catcher's assist, and a run by a batter who reached on a passed ball is unearned. A swinging-strikeout reach is now charged to the right pitcher if he later scores.
  - **Hits and the bases:** on a ground-ball single, runners advance against the outfielder who fields it (it used to be an infielder's arm). Nobody scores on a play whose third out is a force out, a hit can no longer record a 4th out, and once the lead runner is thrown out at home the runners behind him stop. A ground ball with one out can no longer be a triple play (it recorded a 4th out).
  - **Triples:** fast runners still hit the most triples, but the gap is closer to MLB's: an 85-speed burner now hits about 6 per 600 plate appearances instead of about 11 (MLB's fastest hit 7-10), and league-wide triples in a fast league fall from .22 to about .17 per team per game.
  - **The rest of the plate appearance:** when the third out is made on the bases (a caught stealing or pickoff) or a walk-off wild pitch or steal of home ends the game mid at-bat, the batter is not charged a plate appearance and leads off his team's next inning.
  - **Watch a game** shows baserunning plays in words (steals 2nd, caught stealing 3rd, wild pitch, strike three gets away) instead of codes.
  - **Coming next (built, switched off):** fewer extra bases taken, runners who hold on shallow fly balls, more ground-ball double plays and faster hitters beating out infield hits. It became the default in 7.49.0.
  - Scoring is unchanged (calibration 4.32 runs per team-game vs 4.34). The calibration check now gates steal volume and success, wild pitches and passed balls, and five "never happens" rules (steals on fouls, wild pitches with the bases empty, two-out double steals, illegal dropped-third-strike reaches, runs on inning-ending plays), on both test leagues.
- **Release 4b (7.49.0): the bases after contact.** The second half of the running-game work, built in 7.48.0 and switched on now that it passes the calibration checks on its own.
  - **Extra bases at MLB's rate.** Runners took the extra base on a hit about 68% of the time; now about 38-42% (MLB 40%). First to third on a single is about 29% and scoring from second on a single about 60% (they were about 75% and 85%), and runners go on contact with two out. Fast runners and weak outfield arms still take more. Far fewer runners are thrown out trying (about 2% of chances, down from 10%).
  - **Tag-ups are a real race.** A runner on third used to score on 95% of fly outs whoever was running and wherever the ball was caught. Now it depends on how deep the fly is, how fast the runner is and the outfielder's arm: he scores on about 73% of outfield flies, holds on about 25% and is thrown out on 2-3%. Nobody tags on a pop-up or an infield line drive. Sacrifice flies fall to MLB's rate. A runner thrown out tagging up is scored as a double play.
  - **Ground balls with a runner on third.** The runner now scores on most double plays turned with nobody out and is forced home with the bases loaded (he used to hold about 85% of the time). Late in a close game the defence plays the infield in and cuts the run down at the plate more often. Runners on second move up on balls to the right side.
  - **More ground-ball double plays, and speed matters.** Double plays rise to MLB's level (about 0.69 per team per game, up from 0.58). The fastest batters now ground into roughly half as many double plays as the slowest.
  - **Fast hitters beat out infield hits.** Speed now adds hits on softly hit ground balls: the fastest fifth of hitters bat about 25-35 points higher on balls in play than the slowest fifth, and league-wide average is unchanged.
  - **Steals** stay at MLB's level (the base rates were nudged up 5% to offset the extra double plays).
  - Scoring rises slightly (calibration 4.42 runs per team-game, from 4.33; MLB 4.47) because far fewer runners are thrown out on the bases. The calibration check now gates extra bases taken, sacrifice flies and double plays. The old behaviour stays available for one release behind tuning switches (`scripts/kpi_profiles/r4a_legacy.json`).

- **Release 5 (7.51.0): the updated game engine (plate discipline, contact, platoon and game situations).** Nothing changes for a season already under way. Each league moves to the updated game engine at the start of its own next season: when it advances into the new season, before its first regular-season game. A brand-new league starts on it. When a league moves, one league news item says so, and the commissioner's Physics Tuning page says which engine the league is on. No season's stats are split between two engines. When your league moves, expect scoring to come down by about half a run per team-game, to MLB's level, with a few more strikeouts and fewer walks: on a 30-team test league with bunched ratings, like alpha-test's (420 games on each engine through the real game path; sampling error about +/-0.11 runs), runs per team-game went from 4.95 to 4.40 (MLB 4.47), strikeouts from 20.8% to 22.0% of plate appearances and walks from 9.5% to 8.2%.
  - **When a league moves.** A league whose regular season has started finishes it on the engine it is playing now, with results unchanged to the last digit, and moves when it advances into its next season. A league that has already advanced into a new season but has not played a regular-season game moves at that season's first game. Regenerating the schedule or resetting to Opening Day does not change a season's engine.
  - **The rest of what you will see** (same league and sample, today's engine against the updated one): home runs per team-game from 1.03 to 0.99; intentional walks from none to about 12 per team per season (about 15 on the test leagues); hit batters from 1.4% to 0.9% of plate appearances; home teams win .529 where today they win .490 (sampling error about +/-.024).
  - **Why scoring comes down: ratings are read against the league.** Today's engine reads the ratings that decide a plate appearance as absolute numbers, which runs hot in live leagues. On the updated engine every one of them is read relative to the league: a hitter's Contact, Power and Eye against the average of the league's lineup regulars (the hitters who actually bat), and a pitcher's control, movement, pitch grades and arm against the average of the league's staffs weighted by workload (a rotation starter counts 2.5 times a reliever). A league whose players are all rated higher or lower than another's plays the same baseball; what matters is how a player compares with his own league. Tested directly: adding the same amount to every hitter's ratings in a league gave an identical season. The averages are fixed when the season starts. Scoring lands at about MLB's level on all three test leagues: 4.35, 4.45 and 4.52 runs per team-game against the 4.47 target (they were 4.39, 4.13 and 4.37 before ratings were read this way).
  - **What league averages do not even out.** Two differences between leagues remain. A league whose hitters' pull and ground-ball tendencies are bunched together, as alpha-test's are, hits about 7 to 10% fewer home runs than a league with the full spread (1.00 per team-game on a test league built that way and 1.02 on a copy of alpha-test, against 1.10 and 1.09 on the two test fixtures); the next engine release reworks batted balls. And its late innings show almost no extra difficulty for hitters, because its relievers are not rated above its starters and the fresh-arm edge by itself is small; on the two fixtures late-and-close hitting runs .027 and .039 of OPS below normal.
  - **Contact is a sharper skill.** Against an average pitcher an 80 Contact hitter strikes out about 4% of the time and a 65 about 9% (today 11% and 14%); a 50 about 24% and a 35 about half the time. (The owner's target for an 80 was 3%; the last point is on the backlog.) A good contact hitter is also rung up looking less often; today Contact has no say in called third strikes. An ace's strikeout edge now applies equally to every hitter (today elite-contact hitters got only about half of it). Contact also earns a small edge on balls in play, about +.005 of batting average on balls in play per 10 points (the owner chose +.010; it was trimmed because at .010 too many hitters batted .300), and Contact tracks batting average clearly better (correlation about .62, was about .49).
  - **Eye and Control decide walks.** Eye is worth about +2.5 points of walk rate per 10 points (today +0.7): roughly 11.5% for a 60 Eye hitter and 6.5% for a 40. Live leagues will see a modest change until a later release widens the Eye ratings themselves, because generated Eye ratings are bunched together. A pitcher's walks follow his Control (about -1.3 points of walk rate per 10 points, today -0.8), and a tired pitcher now walks more hitters, because Control works through where the pitch goes.
  - **Hitters swing by the count.** The swing rate is within about .04 of MLB's 2023 rate at all twelve counts. Today it is about the same in every count before two strikes: 24% on 3-0 against MLB's 10%, and 36% on 3-1 against MLB's 54%. Two-strike foul balls fall to about .25 per two-strike pitch (MLB .24, today .33), so pitches per plate appearance stay at about 3.85.
  - **Umpires, catchers and hit batters.** Balls and strikes at the edge of the zone are no longer certain, and a good defensive catcher earns extra strikes: about 5 runs a season for every 10 points of catcher fielding, measured against the league's average catcher (an estimate from the size of the effect, not a measured season total). A batter is hit only by a pitch that actually runs in on him, never by one in the strike zone (today almost half were). Wild pitchers hit more batters, and same-handed hitters are hit about twice as often as opposite-handed ones, as in MLB.
  - **Intentional walks exist:** about 15 per team per season on the test leagues (MLB's rate; about 12 in the live-path sample), with first base open and a runner in scoring position, mostly late in close games, and only when the hitter is clearly better than the man on deck. Lineup protection now matters.
  - **Platoon.** Right-handed hitters' platoon split is smaller than left-handed hitters', and each league's split is sized from its own mix of batting sides, so that left- and right-handed pitchers with the same ratings are equally good on average in every league (today left-handers ran up to about 0.5 runs per nine worse in a league heavy with right-handed hitters). Switch hitters get the full platoon edge from either side. The home-run platoon edge no longer jumps for hitters of middling Power. Games, pinch-hitting, the bullpen's matchup choice and the lineup auto-fill all read the platoon edge the same way.
  - **Home field.** Home teams win about 53% of the time, the same for every club and park, in the playoffs too (the All-Star game is unaffected). Measured .531, .540 and .536 on the three test leagues and .529 in the live-path sample (sampling error about +/-.024 there); the owner asked for 53.5%. Today there is no home edge at all.
  - **Late innings and the second time through the order.** A reliever is a little tougher for his first four batters (about 0.2 runs per nine at equal ratings, gone by the seventh batter); the pitcher who starts the game never gets it, whatever his role. Pitchers no longer nibble with first base open and nobody in scoring position, or in every late, close game; both habits were adding walks. On the two test fixtures late and close plate appearances run .027 and .039 of OPS below normal (MLB .02 to .04); a league whose relievers are not rated above its starters shows almost none of it (above). Hitters gain about 9 to 12 points of wOBA the second time they see a starter (MLB about 10; today about 2); the third time is unchanged for now.

  - **Safety.** A backup of the scale 1 players file (and of open draft pools) is kept at the move. A league is **refused** and stays where it is (the commissioner is told, each season until fixed) if converting would push ANY player's rating above 99, or would raise to 1 a rating of a player on an active or injured major-league roster; a rating raised to 1 on a minor leaguer or free agent is allowed and logged (alpha-test has one, a Low-A draft pick's Power). - **8.1.0 fixes for every league (live now):**
  - **A player on a club's injured list (or anywhere on its roster, or under contract with it) is no longer shown as a free agent, and no club can sign him** -- not CPU free agency, a negotiation, the preseason bidding window or a direct signing. Before this, another club could sign an injured player, which replaced his contract, and once he healed he played for both clubs.
  - **The game refuses any roster change that would put a player on a second club**, and says which club holds him. A trade that cannot be completed now changes nothing: its draft picks stay where they were, and the owner can fix the problem and try again.
  - **An open free-agent negotiation for a player who belongs to a club now closes as "not a free agent".** A player who accepts his club's qualifying offer goes straight back onto that club's roster; a player under contract with no roster spot can be put back by his club with Sign, and no other club can sign him.
  - **Parallel game days no longer lose part of a player's stats**: a day on which a player could appear in two games is played one game at a time, so results are identical either way. Box scores show substitutes at a consistent position.
  - **Pitchers page tabs and overall colours** as in 8.0.0; **a combined no-hitter is one news item**; the full-scale tutorial's home-field figure now says 53 to 55%, matching what generation 6 plays.
  - New read-only commissioner tool `scripts/find_double_rostered_players.py`: lists any player on two clubs with a suggested fix, and can write a repaired copy of the changed files to a separate folder for checking before upload.
- **8.1.0: the ball-flight engine (engine generation 7), shipped switched OFF.** Nothing changes for any league until operations turn on `NEXGEN_GENERATION7` (it works only with the 8.0.0 switch on). Then each league moves at the start of its own next season, as with 8.0.0; a league still on the earlier rating scale is converted to the full scale in the same step, and a league created with both switches on starts on it.
  - **Real ball flight.** Every ball in play has a speed off the bat, an angle and a direction, and it flies: air slows it and thin air at altitude lets it carry further. It is a home run only if it clears that park's own fence and wall.
  - **Real ballparks.** The 30 current major-league parks (with the newer fences at Camden Yards, Rogers Centre, Comerica and Kauffman), the Athletics' and Rays' former and temporary homes and the one-off venues play on their real dimensions and walls; any other park plays the average major-league park. Each park gives up home runs the way its real one does, separately for left- and right-handed batters (Baseball Savant 2024-2026). In a league on the new engine, Team Settings also offers Sutter Health Park and George M. Steinbrenner Field, and the Ballparks page shows the dimensions the new engine plays.
  - **Batted balls like MLB's:** ground balls, line drives, fly balls and pop-ups at about MLB's mix, hard-hit balls and barrels at about MLB's rates, and foul pop-ups caught for outs (about 0.8 a team-game).
  - **Fielders run balls down:** whether a ball is an out, a single, a double or a triple depends on where it lands and who can get there, so range and speed show up on every ball; a ball high off a tall wall (Fenway's Green Monster) is a double, not a home run.
  - **Ground-ball pitchers** get more ground balls (about 7 more per 100 balls in play for every 10 points) and give up about 12% fewer home runs.
  - **Scoring stays at MLB's level**: about 4.4 runs per team-game (4.25-4.40 on copies of the live leagues, 4.34-4.67 on the test leagues), about 1.1 home runs, 1.6 doubles and 0.14 triples; a club that does not play in a real park plays the average major-league park. Earlier engines are unchanged to the last digit.
  - **Sliders:** Home Run Rate now sets how far fly balls carry, and Launch Angle Base how high the ball comes off the bat; a setting made for the earlier engine is put back to normal (and kept) when a league moves.
  - **Help:** one news item tells a league when it moves and how many of its clubs play in real parks; Help -> Tutorials -> "The Updated Game Engine" shows the ball-flight steps only to a league on it; the owner/admin guide has the commissioner's summary; the operator steps are `docs/generation7_switch_runbook.md` (park data upload first).
- **8.1.1 fixes:**
  - **Playoff games no longer count in the regular-season stats.** They had been added into every player's and club's season totals (alpha-test's champions read 175 games and 100-75), which also fed the awards, career totals and records at the rollover. Playoff games now keep their own postseason totals, which are saved with the season at the rollover and start empty the next year. The 2026 totals of alpha-test and stats-test were separated exactly from the copy saved when the regular season ended, so no playoff game is lost: each league's 2026 regular-season stats are back to 162 games, and its playoff games are in its 2026 postseason totals. Pages to show the postseason totals come next.
  - **A trade now moves each player's contract to his new club.** Payroll is added up by the contract's club, so a traded player kept being paid by the club that traded him away. alpha-test's 23 traded players' contracts now name the club they play for; nothing else in them changed.
  - Resetting a league to Opening Day (or resetting its stats) also empties its postseason totals.
- **8.1.2 fixes (before the offseason):**
  - **Day-to-day injuries end.** A day-to-day knock never cleared, so a player stayed "injured" for good (alpha-test had 80; 17 of them sat on the 15-day list, where nothing ever brought them back). It now ends after its few days; a player parked on an injured list with one comes back like any other returner (a CPU club activates him; an owner's club follows the owner's injured-list settings).
  - **The offseason heals.** At the rollover, every injury that would end before next opening day is over, and returners come back under the same rules; longer injuries (an elbow reconstruction due back in July) carry into the season.
  - **Owners are told what to fill.** After the rollover, each owner whose club has open roster spots gets a news item and a notification saying how many, and what (if anything) must be fixed before Opening Day (for example a position nobody covers). In the offseason and preseason the Roster page shows a "spots to fill" banner with the club's counts and a link to free agency. An open spot is still legal; the CPU never signs for an owner's club.
  - **The Players page no longer lists retired players as free agents**: they show as "Retired" (with a Hide retired option), and a player under contract with no roster spot shows under his club as "No spot". "Free agents only" now lists exactly the players on the Free Agency page.
  - **Injured free agents show an "Injured" badge** on the Free Agency page, with the injury and expected return date.
  - **An offer made with no salary entered uses the player's asking price**, so older free agents no longer counter it. A direct signing with no length given now gets the length the player accepted, not one year.
- **8.1.3: owners are told who left their club.** At the rollover each owner's notice (news and notification) now names who retired and whose contract expired, and a club that lost players but has no spots to fill still hears who left. In the offseason and preseason the Roster page lists the same players; each expired-contract player links to his page, where the club can make him an offer to bring him back. A rehearsal of alpha-test's rollover on a copy (2026-10-18): 240 contracts expired, 62 players retired, 88 day-to-day injuries healed, and all seven owner clubs got a named notice.
