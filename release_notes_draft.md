## 7.1.0
# 7.1 Release Notes — CPU League Realism (Sprint 2)
## CPU roster management
- **Deadline-aware CPU trading:** contenders buy (chase veterans), rebuilders
- **CPU-to-CPU trades:** CPU teams now trade with each other, not just with you
- **In-season callups + September expansion:** CPU teams promote AAA prospects
## On-field realism (physics engine)
- Platoon-aware lineups vs LHP/RHP and a modern slot-weighted batting order.
- Pitch-count-based reliever rest, closers used in tied 9th innings/extras, and
- Times-through-order penalty (batters improve on later looks), pinch-hitters
- New KPI gates hold league AVG/OBP/SLG, K%/BB%, home-run and usage
## Fixes
- League history and the leagues list endpoints fixed; assorted stability and

- Finance: new Payroll vs Luxury Threshold card on the Finance page - visual floor/payroll/threshold meter, headroom, estimated tax/floor fee, and Opening Day solvency status, using the same math settlement applies.
- Free Agency: offer dialog now previews the signing's impact on your books - payroll before/after vs the luxury threshold, estimated tax, cash remaining after the signing bonus (with debt-accrual warning), and Opening Day solvency risk.
- API: added GET /teams/{team_id}/finance/payroll-context and a payroll_impact block on the free-agent evaluate-offer response.
- Contracts: salary column now shows compact values with total commitment (e.g. 8.5M/yr, 25.5M over 3 yrs), service time reads as years and days, and the Expiring / Arb-eligible / options / Non-gtd badges explain themselves on hover.
- Finance settings: each module level dropdown (league creation + commissioner) now explains what the selected level actually changes.
- Finance Queue: rows now show player names, plain-language decisions, and salary movement instead of raw ids/tokens, and Apply Approved shows a confirmation summary of exactly what will be committed.
- Help: tutorials and manuals updated for the new finance UX - Finance Hub tutorial covers the Payroll vs Luxury Threshold card and the Contracts tracker, Free Agency tutorial covers the live signing-impact panel, admin tutorial covers the humanized Finance Queue, and the Finance System Manual section 8 now describes hybrid enforcement instead of the retired warn/block modes.
- UI fixes: the standings button in the status ribbon and the Season page's regenerate-schedule link now navigate correctly; commissioner-only pages consistently redirect owners; deleting a league uses the in-app confirm dialog; the command palette now lists every page (filtered by role) instead of a stale hand-kept list.
- Performance: team logos and player avatars are now fetched once and shared across the whole session instead of once per table row; initial page load is ~40 percent smaller (Firebase now loads only when cloud sign-in is used); server auth no longer blocks concurrent requests (membership lookups are cached).
- Simulation: breaking and offspeed pitches now arrive slower than fastballs (per-type velocity), and the KPI harness gates many more realism metrics (slash line, contact quality, first-pitch strikes, ISO, batted-ball mix).
- Performance: season simulation is another ~2.6x faster (240-game benchmark 23.6s to ~9s) via day-batched stats/recovery persistence, shared file caches, and engine inner-loop optimizations - with byte-identical simulation results verified at every step.
- Performance: faster page loads - league contract tracker no longer rescans roster files per row, player profiles read the finance ledger once instead of twice, cloud saves sync only the active league's files, and big tables (players, contracts, stats, schedule) use virtualized rendering.
- Fix: schedule rows now store boxscore file paths instead of embedding full HTML into schedule.csv - fixes broken boxscore links from the Schedule page and stops the schedule file from growing by megabytes over a season.
- Pitching (7.38.0): a pitcher's declared role (SP/RP) now outranks the endurance guess. Leagues whose pitchers sit just under the old endurance cutoff had no recognised starters at all (alpha-test: 0 of 460; now 252).
- Leaders (7.38.1): league leaderboards now show each player's team.
- Trades (7.39.0): you can counter any offer, including one from another owner, not just CPU offers. The counter dialog uses searchable roster pickers and the live acceptance meter instead of typed player ids.
- Teams (7.39.1): every team page has a full roster browser - Active, AAA, LOW and injured-list tabs with position, age, bats/throws and overall - so you can scout a trade partner's whole organisation.
- Pitching (7.40.0): starters are now rested and budgeted as starters, using your own SP1-SP5 staff assignments. Every pitcher had been stored with a stale "RP" role, which gave starters reliever rest. Starter selection can also be logged for diagnosis (NEXGEN_ROTATION_DIAGNOSTICS).
- Pitching (7.41.0): the game now starts the pitcher the rotation actually chose. The engine had been re-picking a starter from a counter that reset on every sim run, so each weekly run replayed SP1, SP2, SP3, SP4, SP5, SP1, SP2 and the top two starters got roughly double the starts of the other three.
- Pitching (7.42.0): when one of your starters is on the injured list, his rotation spot goes to a pitcher who can actually start - your long man before your closer, never the reverse - and a poor fill no longer sticks for the rest of the season.
- Team stats (7.43.0): Team Totals are now the sum of the players currently on your roster, since season stats travel with a player when he is traded. Your club's W-L record is shown separately and is unchanged. Averages and ERA are recomputed from their components rather than averaged.
- Draft (7.44.0): drafts with human owners now work. "Advance to next owner" runs the CPU teams and stops when an owner is up, and every advance button now stops at owners. An optional pick clock lets the CPU pick for an owner whose time runs out, with an on-the-clock post to Discord and a live countdown on the draft page.
- Fix (7.44.0): trade offers sent to another owner were being answered automatically by the CPU. Team ownership is now read from league membership, not the empty owner column in teams.csv.
- Draft (7.44.1): the draft only runs by itself once a pick clock is set. At 0 it never advances on its own; the commissioner drives it.
- Draft (7.44.2): the commissioner can make or auto-pick the selection for whichever team is on the clock ("Pick for TEAM"), so an owner who isn't responding can't stall the draft.
- Fix (7.44.3): an amateur draft in progress now keeps the season paused. Previously, once a league had made even one pick, the next scheduled run resumed the regular season and simulated straight through the draft.
- Draft (7.44.4): new **My picks** card on the Draft page (Now and History tabs) lists every player your team has drafted with a position strip (C, 1B, 2B, 3B, SS, LF, CF, RF, P) counting what you have taken, so empty positions stand out before your next pick. A team picker lets you view any team's picks. Draft tutorial updated, including the 7.44 pick clock, Advance to next owner, and commissioner Pick for TEAM.
- Draft (7.44.5): the commissioner's **Auto-finish draft** works again. It makes every remaining pick, owners included, after a confirmation listing whose picks will be made; since 7.44.0 it had stopped at the first owner and made no picks. It is now commissioner-only, and the server refuses an owner's request to auto-pick for other owners.
- Player profiles (7.44.6): the strikeout column (K) now shows for every hitter and pitcher; it read a field the simulation never writes, so it was blank for everyone. Pitchers' win percentage and opponents' batting average are now filled in, the always-blank DERA column is replaced by FIP, ERA/FIP/WHIP read to two decimals, and innings show in baseball notation (70.1, not 70.333).
- Simulation (7.44.6): pinch runners are now used only for the tying or go-ahead run. Bench speedsters had been sent in for almost any slow runner from the 7th inning on, even with the team trailing by eight; over 20 simulated days bench appearances fell from 2.16 to 0.94 per team-game, and specialists who were used in about 85% of games now appear in 15-45% of them. Those extra pinch runners had been worth about 0.08 runs a game, so the overall run environment is nudged back up to compensate (offense_scale 1.015 to 1.0175); league scoring stays where it was and the calibration gates pass on both reference seeds.
- Simulation (7.45.0): **power now hits for power.** The Power rating had almost no effect on home runs while Contact drove them (HR rate vs Power r = -0.06, vs Contact +0.77 on alpha-test), so high-average, ordinary-power hitters posted sluggers' numbers. Contact now decides whether and how squarely the ball is met; Power decides how hard it is hit, with a curve that leaves average hitters average and lets genuine sluggers separate. League scoring and home-run totals are unchanged; who hits the home runs is not. On a 30-day sim of alpha-test, HR vs Power went from +0.01 to +0.45 and the home-run leaders went from contact hitters with power ratings of 48-52 to sluggers rated 60-63. The calibration harness now gates that ratings drive the outcomes they name.
- Fix (7.45.1): a file-permission helper written for Windows (`os.chmod(path, stat.S_IWRITE)`) stripped read and execute access from every league data directory on Linux, so nothing inside could be opened. Production was unaffected only because Cloud Run runs as root; it is what crashed the calibration check on GitHub. It now adds owner-write and leaves the rest of the mode alone. The calibration CI check also now runs at all: it had failed every one of its 64 runs since January, first on committed scratch symlinks under tmp/, then on a stale requirements.txt name, then on this.
- Fix (7.45.2): drafted players no longer disappear from rosters. A shared ownership registry for drafted players was kept once per server process instead of once per league, and draft ids repeat across leagues (D20260013 exists in every 2026 draft), so another league's draft decided who owned alpha-test's picks and they were silently dropped on roster load. Each league now has its own registry, the roster files are the authority on who a player plays for, and entries naming teams from other leagues are purged.
- Rosters (7.45.3): the CPU never trims an owner's team. When a deadline passes, an owner's roster is only fixed where it is illegal ("fill gaps"); nobody is released unless the owner runs Auto-reassign themselves. And no player from this year's draft class is released just for room on any team: auto-assign releases the weakest non-draftee instead, or keeps the pick and leaves the team over the limit.
- Trades (7.45.4): trading a minor leaguer now takes him off his old team. The trade screen offers AAA, Low-A and injured players, but the trade only removed the player from the old team's active roster, so he ended up listed by both clubs (HOU's Erich Zenk was on HOU's and BAL's AAA rosters for a month after HOU traded him). For a traded draft pick it was worse: the next roster reload dropped him from his new team and the trade was silently undone. Draft picks in a trade are also now transferred before any roster changes, so a rejected pick cannot leave a half-applied trade behind, and the transaction log records the level the player actually left.
- Reliability (7.45.5): copied files are now saved to cloud storage. The cloud server decided what to save by comparing each file's modified time with the time of the last save, and a copied file keeps its source's time, so a copy was invisible to it: the season-end archive (last season's players, stats, playoffs and draft files), a cloned league, and the start-of-season roster snapshot would have existed only on the running server and vanished at the next restart. It now tracks every file's modified time and size since its last save and saves any file that is new or different. The server also saves anything still unsaved when it shuts down for a deploy or restart, unless a sim or other background job is mid-write, in which case it leaves the stored league as it was rather than save it half-updated.
- Simulation (7.45.6): **big-league games are played by the active roster.** Since 3.2.12 a game's player pool was the whole organisation -- AAA, Low-A and injured players included -- so the default lineup was the best nine hitters at any level and benches and bullpens ran 20+ deep. League creation wrote those lineups to disk, so on alpha-test ten CPU clubs started three to five minor leaguers in every game of the season, and a player on the 15-day DL played all 71. Games now use the 25-man active roster only; injured players are never eligible, and healthy minor leaguers fill in only if the active roster cannot field nine hitters or a pitcher. A saved lineup that names someone not on the active roster is rebuilt from the active roster before the game, as before.
- Pitching staff (7.45.6): a role held by a pitcher who can't pitch -- traded, released, sent down or hurt -- now shows as vacant on the Lineups page, with who held it and why, instead of keeping him in the slot. Trades never updated the old team's staff, so seven alpha-test clubs still listed a pitcher they no longer had, and saving the staff failed until the owner found and removed him.
- Pitchers (7.45.7): a pitcher's SP/RP now reads the same on every page. players.csv's stored role column is left over from an old endurance-only rule and says "RP" for almost every pitcher in existing leagues (492 of 494 on alpha-test, starters included). The roster page already ignored it, but the player profile, Free Agency and the players list showed it as-is, so a starter could be SP on one page and RP on the next. They now derive it like the roster page: the pitcher's declared role first, then endurance. The dashboard's bullpen card also counted every rotation starter as a reliever for the same reason; it now follows your pitching staff.
- Box scores (7.45.8): box score links are now stored relative to the league (`boxscores/season/<game>.html`) instead of as the server's own file path (`/work/data/leagues/<league>/data/...`). A league that is cloned, restored on another machine or moved to a new storage mount keeps working box score links. Links already stored in the old form keep working too: the box score viewer finds the file by its place in the league's box score folder, wherever the path came from.
- Sims (7.45.9): **a sim day can no longer stall on "Player X is not on the active roster."** When a player got hurt, the game promoted whoever stood first in AAA -- a pitcher on most CPU clubs -- so active rosters slowly turned into 8 hitters and 17 pitchers. Below nine hitters the lineup fill reached across the league for other teams' minor leaguers, the game rejected them, and the day stopped on every retry, losing the rest of that day's games. Fixed at every step:
  - **Injuries use the depth chart first** (commissioner's rule). The next healthy player behind the injured one who is already active takes his lineup spot; only that spot changes. An owner's open roster spot stays open for the owner. A call-up happens for an owner's club only if nobody active can play the position, and then only someone who can. CPU clubs call up like for like (a hitter for a hitter) from their own organisation, and send that same player back down when the regular returns.
  - **Every club gets a depth chart.** Clubs that never saved one get one generated at the next sim (17 of alpha-test's 20). A chart a person saved is never changed; a generated one guides injury coverage but does not pick starters.
  - **Emergency call-ups only from your own organisation**, recorded, when a club can't field nine position players -- before the day's games, owner clubs included, so a game is never unplayable.
  - **CPU clubs keep sound active rosters**: at least 12 position players, a healthy catcher, full and under the cap, every move recorded. CPU clubs also bring players back from the 60-day list (nobody ever did).
  - **A day that fails partway** keeps the games it finished, and the retry plays only the missing ones (it used to drop them and could double-count stats). A retried day no longer skips a starter's turn.
  - Automatic call-ups and send-downs now appear in the Transactions log; loading a roster no longer silently refills it.
  - Fixed: pitcher rest and rotation state was shared by every league on the same server; secondary positions stored as "SS|3B" or "['RF']" weren't recognised.
- Stats (7.45.10): caught stealing, pickoffs, putouts and catcher's interference are no longer double-counted. Batting and fielding stats shared four names in season stats, so every caught stealing was stored twice (league SB% read .718; the real figure is .836), catchers showed 40-plus "caught stealing" as runners, and a first baseman's putouts landed in "picked off". Fixed for games from this release on; stats already recorded this season stand (commissioner decision).
- Box scores (7.45.10): line scores show every inning -- extra-inning games used to stop at the 9th, so the inning runs didn't add up to the final score. Save opportunities are now saves plus blown saves (they were counted at every save-situation entry).
- Fielding credit (7.45.10): pitchers no longer get an assist on every strikeout (a strikeout is the catcher's putout), bunt outs are credited to whoever fielded the bunt instead of always the shortstop, and the double-play pivot man gets his assist. Fielding totals only -- no game results change. Season fielding totals mix the old and new crediting for the rest of 2026.
- OVR (7.45.10): the overall rating now reflects how players actually perform. Hitters are rated mainly on contact and power, plus a little eye, speed and a position-weighted defense value (shortstops and centre fielders count most); pitchers on control, movement and the pitches they actually throw -- a pitcher no longer gains rating just for having more pitches. CPU roster moves and trades use the same rating, so CPU teams now value pitchers on the same scale as hitters. Expect OVR and stars to change; in leagues with tightly bunched ratings the raw score is shown rather than a stretched one. Prospect promotions are unchanged.
- Ballparks (7.45.10): a real ballpark's dimensions apply only when that park was chosen for the team, not because a generated stadium name happens to match a real park ("Royals Stadium" was getting Kauffman Stadium's 1973-93 fences). Applies to new leagues; existing teams keep their current parks.

- Tooling (7.45.11, audit Release 2; no change to game results): the calibration check now measures far more of what the engine audit found. Steal benchmarks are corrected to MLB's real rate (they were about 2x), the 30- and 40-homer counts are realistic and can fail on the low side, and new metrics cover hard-hit, barrel and sweet-spot rates and extra bases taken. About 100 report-only KPIs (bullpen usage, swing rates by count, walk/strikeout spread, velocity fade, run expectancy by base-out state, runs per inning, late & close, platoon by hand, home-field edge, team talent spread) each come with an MLB reference. Known misses are reported on every run without failing it, so each fix release can be measured. A second test league, `data/calibration_league`, is built the way a real league is (league creation, auto-assign, pitching and lineup auto-fill) and runs in CI as report-only. Each plate appearance's first pitch-log entry now records the inning, outs, runners and score; a new test shows simming one day at a time loses bullpen rest (fixed in Release 3); and the injury check now counts injured-list stints only, which shows pitchers land on the IL about 9x less often than in MLB.
- **Rosters (7.46.0): MLB's 26-man active roster.** Teams now carry up to 26 active players, with at most 13 pitchers (28 and 14 from September 1). The organisation limit is 51 (26 active + 15 AAA + 10 Low-A; injured lists don't count), so nobody has to release anyone. Every league switches over automatically; a 25-man roster is still legal.
  - **Your team:** nothing is moved for you. The Roster page shows the limits in force today, your pitcher and position-player counts, and a "spot is open" badge (for example ACT 25/26); the Season page lists the open spot under "Needs your attention", and you get a notify-only "Active roster spot open" notification when you sim. Fill it by promoting from AAA or signing a free agent.
  - **13-pitcher limit:** calling up, signing or trading for a 14th active pitcher goes through with a warning, so you can promote first and option someone next. A roster swap that leaves 14 is refused, and the next game won't start until you're back to 13. "Fill gaps only" fixes it by optioning a reliever (never a rotation starter, never a release). A pitcher coming off the injured list onto a full staff goes to AAA.
  - **CPU clubs** build 13 pitchers / 13 position players from their own AAA and Low-A before each game day and once at Opening Day, never touch an owner's roster, and stop entirely if team ownership can't be read. Every move is in the Transactions log.
  - **September:** rosters may expand to 28 with up to 14 pitchers. When the playoffs begin, every team (owners included) is optioned back to 26 and 13 pitchers, never released; if AAA is full, a young player goes to Low-A instead.
  - **Pitching staff:** still 11 slots. The 12th and 13th pitchers are extra relievers (the Lineups page lists them); more relief slots come with the bullpen update (Release 3).
  - **Offseason:** only CPU clubs promote prospects automatically; yours get a news suggestion and the decision is yours.
  - **New leagues** start with 26-man rosters (13/13), 51-player organisations and editor-valid pitching staffs.
  - **Auto-assign:** a full reassign of an organisation over 51 releases only the players over the limit; anyone else without a slot is parked in AAA and shown as overflow for you to trim. "Fill gaps only" may promote your best AAA bat into an open active spot when AAA is over its cap.
  - **New tutorial:** Help → Tutorials → "26-Man Active Roster". The Roster tutorial, manual and owner guide are updated.
  - Fixed along the way: September swaps on a 27-28-man roster were rejected as over a 25-man cap; the commissioner's "CPU-fill this team" could release an owner's players (it now only fixes what is illegal); "auto-assign all teams" now reassigns CPU teams only and reports the owner teams it skipped; the September revert could option an owner's only healthy catcher; a CPU club with a full organisation could keep outbidding an owner for a free agent it could not sign, so the negotiation never closed; the CPU free-agent market and yearly promotions could act on owners' teams if ownership couldn't be read.
  - **Owner safety (all CPU automation):** if the league's list of owners can't be read for a moment, the CPU now does nothing that day instead of treating every club as CPU-run. That covers roster moves, free-agent bids and negotiations, injured-list activations, the draft pick clock, trade replies and offseason arbitration. The owners list is also saved in one step, so it can never be read half-written.
